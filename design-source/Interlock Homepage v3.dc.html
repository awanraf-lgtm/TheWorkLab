01 / The problem

The feature your system never built

Your core platform handles the standard workflow. Everything it cannot do survives as exports, spreadsheets, re-keying, and repetitive checks - simple, deterministic work that quietly grows into thousands of hours as volume increases.
01

The export

Someone downloads a CSV every Tuesday because the system's report is not the report the team
actually needs.
02

The side calculation

One person maintains a spreadsheet of formulas and adjustments that drives decisions the core
system cannot make - or even see.
03

The re-key

The final result is manually entered into another system because the two platforms were never
built to work together.

An AI tool bought against the documented 70% automates work that was already working.

02 / The method

The judgment your system cannot make

Some work cannot be reduced to fixed rules: reprioritizing requests, reassigning work, and choosing the next best action as conditions change. These decisions require context and adaptation, but not deep expertise - so people keep making the same low-level judgment calls by hand.
01

The daily reprioritization

Every new request forces someone to reshuffle the backlog by urgency, capacity, and customer
02

The manual reassignment

When availability or priorities change, someone has to decide who should take what next.
03

The next-best-action decision

A person repeatedly weighs customer history, current conditions, and business priorities to choose
what happens next.
Stage 01 · Day 1–2

Observe

Two days sitting beside the team while they work. Not interviews — an interview returns the job description, and the job description is the part we already know.

Gate — your SME confirms what we saw
Stage 02 · Day 4–6

Decompose

Every role broken into the discrete tasks it actually performs — seeded from a standard occupation library, then corrected against what we watched. The library is scaffolding, not the answer; the gap between it and reality is the finding.

Gate — your SME validates the task list
Stage 03 · Day 6–7

Classify

Each task sorted into Automate, Augment, Human-led or Leave — with the reasoning attached to every call, so you can disagree with a specific one rather than the whole report.

Gate — your SME validates the classifications
Stage 04 · Day 7–10

Score

Effort weighed against business impact and computed in code, not by a model. Identical inputs give an identical score every time, and we show the working — because this is the number you will argue with.

03 / What you get

Four documents.
One will annoy you.

That is the point. Everything is written to be handed to a finance director or an engineer without translation. No maturity curves, no readiness scores, no quadrants.

Deliverable 01

The task register

Every task in the department, classified and scored, with the reasoning attached to each call. Roughly 40 rows per role. This is the artifact everything else is derived from.

Deliverable 02

The handoff map

Every point where data crosses between two systems by human hand, what it costs you in hours per month, and which crossings can be closed without replacing either system.

Deliverable 03

The costed plan

The ten highest-scoring opportunities, each with a build estimate, a payback figure, a named owner and a sequence. Written so you can take it straight into a budget conversation.

Deliverable 04

What not to automate

The work that looks automatable and isn't, and why. Usually the shortest document and always the most argued-over. It is also the one that stops you spending six figures on a pilot that was never going to hold.

04 / Fit

This wastes money
in some departments.

Worth booking if

  • +You run 20–400 people and one department has a budget owner who can approve a change.
  • +The work is operational and repeatable — claims, billing, onboarding, procurement, support.
  • +You already have a system of record, and people have quietly built habits around its gaps.
  • +Someone internal can give us two days of their team's actual working time.

Don't book if

  • −You want a strategy deck to show the board. We produce a task list and a bill of work.
  • −Nobody in the room can authorise a change to how the team works.
  • −The goal is a headcount number to justify a decision already taken. We won't take the engagement.
  • −You need the answer this week. Two days of observation is the part that can't be compressed.
